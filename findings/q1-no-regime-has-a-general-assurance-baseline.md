# No regime answers Q1 "yes" — and the three with a national identity number answer a different question instead

*Cross-regime finding. Corpora read 2026-09-16 from the sibling checkouts named in [`q4-requester-verification-is-not-universal.md`](q4-requester-verification-is-not-universal.md) §0, which is the artefact list for this file too. Answers [`docs/questions.md`](../docs/questions.md) **Q1** — "is there a general identity-assurance baseline, or is assurance set interaction by interaction?"*

`asia-id-strategy.md` §3 predicted that with Japan, Indonesia and India in the set, "for the first time the answer to Q1 may be *yes*". It is not. But the reason it is not is more useful than a yes would have been.

**Conclusion, in five parts.**

1. **No corpus in the programme contains a provision of general application setting an identity-assurance baseline for interactions at large.** Utah's answer — no general baseline, assurance set interaction by interaction — survives contact with eight more regimes. Whoever asserts the universal owes the general provision (`method.md` §7), and across nine corpora nobody has produced one.
2. **A national identity number does not make one.** Japan, Indonesia and India all have a statutory national number, and in all three the number is an *identifier*. What varies is where the proofing sits, and it is never at the point of use.
3. **Two of them are the same shape, which deserves a name: front-loaded proofing.** Assurance is established once, by the state, at registration or enrolment — documentary, IAL2-shaped, and genuinely strong — and everything downstream is assertion against a register. Indonesia's registration is compulsory on every resident; India's enrolment is not. That difference is the whole distance between Indonesia's answer and India's.
4. **Japan is not that shape at all.** Its number is universal, but its verification duty is *transactional* and narrow: it binds a defined class of administrative actors, at the moment they collect a number from the person. In the taxonomy's terms Japan is the closest of the three to Utah — assurance set interaction by interaction, with the interactions enumerated by statute.
5. **Two regimes have a general assurance *vocabulary*, and neither has made it a baseline.** The EU's low/substantial/high binds notified schemes for cross-border public-sector recognition, plus wallets. Thailand's IAL/AAL binds four enumerated licensable services. A vocabulary of general application, bound to a narrow perimeter, is not a baseline — and mistaking one for the other is the single easiest error available on this question.

And one finding about the programme rather than about the law: **Utah's headline needs qualifying against Utah's own corpus.** See §6.

---

## 1. The test, and why it is not "does the regime take identity seriously"

`questions.md` Q1 asks for a provision of general application. `taxonomy.md` §1.1 supplies the discipline that keeps the question honest, because four different duties get conflated under "strong identification":

| Duty | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| **Identity assertion** | A name is on record |
| **Identity proofing** | The claimed identity is real and belongs to the claimant (~NIST IAL2) |
| **Status verification** | An attribute holds |
| **Authentication** | A returning party is the same as the record (~NIST AAL) |

A national number that nobody is obliged to verify is an assertion regime with a big database. So for each regime the question is not "is there a number" but **who is bound, to do which of the four, and when**. Every entry below answers those three.

## 2. Japan — a universal identifier, and a duty that binds a defined class at a defined moment

**The number is universal.** 番号法 assigns a 個人番号 to every person on the resident register. The Act's only general verification provision is §16, `425AC0000000027`, `legislative`, `in-force`:

> (本人確認の措置)
> 第十六条 個人番号利用事務等実施者は、第十四条第一項の規定により本人から個人番号の提供を受けるときは、次の各号のいずれかに掲げる措置をとらなければならない。
> 一 個人番号の提供をする者から個人番号カードの提示を受けること。
> 二 個人番号の提供をする者から第十八条の二第六項の規定によるカード代替電磁的記録の送信を受けるとともに、当該カード代替電磁的記録について同条第七項の規定による確認を行うこと。
> 三 前二号に掲げるもののほか、個人番号の提供をする者が本人であることを確認するための措置として政令で定める措置

*My gloss, not corpus text:* a person executing My-Number-related affairs must, when receiving a My Number from the person themselves under §14(1), take one of three measures — take presentation of the individual number card; receive the phone-borne card-substitute record and perform the §18-2(7) check on it; or a measure prescribed by Cabinet Order for confirming that the person supplying the number is who they say they are.

**It fails the generality test on both axes at once.**

*Who is bound:* 個人番号利用事務等実施者, a defined term — the entities executing or handling number-using administrative affairs. Not the public, and not every recipient of a number.

*When:* only on receipt of the number 「本人から」, from the person themselves, under the §14(1) collection route. There is no clause anywhere in the 40 instruments saying that a 個人番号 holder must be verified to any standard as such.

**The assurance level lives one hop further down, and then a second hop out of reach.** The Cabinet Order fleshing out §16(iii), `426CO0000000155` §12, `delegated`, `in-force`:

> 第十二条 法第十六条第三号の政令で定める措置は、個人番号の提供を行う者から次に掲げる書類の提示を受けることその他これに準ずるものとして主務省令で定める措置とする。

*My gloss:* the measures prescribed by Cabinet Order for §16(iii) are taking presentation of the following documents from the person supplying the number, or measures the competent ministerial ordinance prescribes as equivalent.

So: statute names the duty, Cabinet Order names the documents, ministerial ordinance names the equivalents. The same shape — a photo-document list at 省令 tier — recurs in the two other request procedures quoted in the Q4 finding. There is a de facto common floor in Japanese administrative practice; **no instrument in the corpus states it generally.**

**Layer not searched:** 個人情報保護法 (APPI) is out of `japan-id`'s scope by decision, named in its README Known Gap 4. Japan's general data-protection duties, whatever they say about assurance, are not evidenced here.

## 3. Indonesia — compulsory registration, verification at the registrar, assertion thereafter

**This is the closest thing to a "yes" in the programme, and it is worth setting out precisely because it is close.**

*Everyone must register.* UU 23/2006, `UU-23-2006`, `legislative`, **`amended`**:

> Setiap Penduduk wajib melaporkan Peristiwa Kependudukan dan Peristiwa Penting yang dialaminya kepada Instansi Pelaksana dengan memenuhi persyaratan yang diperlukan dalam Pendaftaran Penduduk dan Pencatatan Sipil.

*My gloss:* every resident must report the population events and vital events they experience to the Implementing Agency, meeting the requirements needed for Population Registration and Civil Registration.

*The registrar must verify.* Same instrument, the duties of the Instansi Pelaksana:

> f. melakukan verifikasi dan validasi data dan informasi yang disampaikan oleh Penduduk dalam pelayanan Pendaftaran Penduduk dan Pencatatan Sipil.

*My gloss:* carrying out verification and validation of the data and information submitted by residents in Population Registration and Civil Registration services.

*And the number is then the universal key.* UU 24/2013, `UU-24-2013`, `legislative`, **`amended`**:

> (2) NIK sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (1) menjadi nomor identitas tunggal untuk semua urusan pelayanan publik.
> (3) Pemerintah menyelenggarakan semua pelayanan publik dengan berdasarkan NIK sebagaimana dimaksud pada ayat (2).

*My gloss:* the NIK becomes the single identity number for all public-service matters, and the Government conducts all public services **on the basis of** the NIK.

**Read the verb.** `berdasarkan` — on the basis of, keyed to. Downstream services are told to use the NIK as the join key. Nothing tells them to check it, and no provision in the corpus sets an assurance level for its presentation.

So Indonesia has a **universal, compulsory, state-performed proofing event** feeding an ecosystem in which the number is thereafter asserted. In the taxonomy's four-way split that is **proofing once, assertion forever** — not a baseline for interactions, and not Utah's interaction-by-interaction pattern either. It is a third shape.

**Three caveats, and the first is severe.**

**Every one of these quotes is from an item the corpus marks `amended`, so none is quotable as current law.** The Adminduk family in `../indonesia-id/corpus` is `UU-23-2006` `amended`, `UU-24-2013` `amended`, `PP-40-2019` `amended` — there is no consolidated current text of Indonesian population-administration law in the corpus at all. `UU-24-2013` is the amending Act and is itself recorded `amended` on a `status_hukum` of `sebagian` with **no amending instrument in the source's own relation graph**, a conflict `indonesia-id`'s README documents and does not resolve. The substance is almost certainly still good — but this finding cannot say so from this corpus, and says instead that Indonesia's Q1 answer currently rests on text the programme's own machinery refuses to certify.

**The operative layer is a ministry regulation nobody can reach.** PP 40/2019 delegates the KTP-el recording-device standard and the reader specification to the Minister. Every `kemendagri.go.id` host black-holes the TCP SYN from this box; Permendagri 72/2022 is unread. `indonesia-id`'s README calls solving that egress "the single highest-value next step".

**Figures and quotes are as read at 2026-09-16T21:30 UTC, HEAD `07e2f14`**, while the repo was being re-harvested.

## 4. India — the same front-loaded proofing, and enrolment is not compulsory

Enrolment proofing is real and documentary. `ENROLMENT-REGS-2016` reg. 10, `delegated`, `in-force`:

> 10. Submission and verification of information. — (1) An individual who seeks to enrol shall be required to submit an application … and shall— (a) present at the enrolment centre, in original, for verification and scanning, one or more documents evidencing proof of identity and proof of address and, where applicable, proof of date of birth or proof of relationship or both, as the case may be; or (b) give his consent for electronically obtaining the information evidencing the proofs referred to in clause (a) from the databases of the authorities dealing with the preparation or maintenance of such information…

**But the use of what it produces is voluntary by the statute's own word.** `AADHAAR-2016-s4`, `legislative`, `in-force` — and the whole of sub-sections (3) to (7) is bracketed `1[…]` as substituted by the Aadhaar and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019, so the voluntariness is the 2019 amendment's doing:

> ¹[(3) Every Aadhaar number holder to establish his identity, **may voluntarily use** his Aadhaar number in physical or electronic form by way of authentication or offline verification, or in such other form as may be notified, in such manner as may be specified by regulations.
>
> Explanation.— For the purposes of this section, voluntary use of the Aadhaar number by way of authentication means the use of such Aadhaar number only with the informed consent of the Aadhaar number holder.

And the compulsion clause is entirely derivative:

> (7) Notwithstanding anything contained in the foregoing provisions, **mandatory authentication** of an Aadhaar number holder for the provision of any service **shall take place if such authentication is required by a law made by Parliament.**]

**So the Aadhaar Act never mandates authentication by its own force.** It creates the infrastructure and defers the compulsion to whatever other Act of Parliament wants it. §4(6) goes further and requires a requesting entity to offer "alternate and viable means of identification" and forbids denial of service for refusing or being unable to authenticate.

**The validity trap, handled.** Two provisions that a naive reading would put at the centre of this answer are not current law in the form they are usually quoted.

- **§57 is `repealed`**, not merely struck down. The 2018 judgment held unconstitutional "that portion of Section 57 of the Aadhaar Act which enables body corporate and individual to seek authentication"; Parliament then omitted the section entire (Act 14 of 2019, s. 25, w.e.f. 25-7-2019). The correct present-tense statement is that it no longer exists. Never "s. 57 provides".
- **§7 is `read-down`**, and its text as written overstates it. The 2018 holding: "the expression 'benefit' has to be read ejusdem generis with the preceding word 'subsidies'. … We also make it clear that a benefit which is earned by an individual (e.g. pension by a government employee) cannot be covered under Section 7 of the Act". So §7 reaches only Consolidated-Fund-charged expenditure, and even there its proviso guarantees an alternate means to anyone without a number.

`aadhaar` derives its manifests' `validity` from a provision-level overlay in `sources/judicial-overlay.tsv`, so `lawcite --in-force-only` does filter these correctly at the Act layer — verified: `Consolidated Fund of India` returns 3 hits over `corpus-acts`, and 0 with the flag, the read-down §7 among the suppressed. **The flag does not protect the delegated layer**, which is instrument-grained and carries superseded wordings inside footnotes of items that are honestly `in-force`; every delegated-layer hit there was read in context.

**India is therefore front-loaded proofing with an opt-in front door.** Strong proofing at enrolment, an authentication infrastructure nobody is obliged to use, and a statutory right to an alternative. That is further from a general baseline than Indonesia, not closer.

## 5. Assurance vocabularies that are not baselines: the EU and Thailand

### 5.1 The EU has the vocabulary, and it is scoped twice over

eIDAS defines the three levels, `02014R0910-20241018` (consolidated, `legislative`, `in-force`), Art. 8:

> **Article 8 — Assurance levels of electronic identification schemes**
>
> 1. An electronic identification scheme notified pursuant to Article 9(1) shall specify assurance levels low, substantial and/or high for electronic identification means issued under that scheme.

**Article 8(1) is the scope clause and it is the whole answer.** The levels are a property of *notified schemes*. Nothing in the article reaches identification that is not done under one.

The second scoping is in Art. 6, "Mutual recognition":

> When an electronic identification using an electronic identification means and authentication is required under national law or by administrative practice to access a service provided by a public sector body online in one Member State, the electronic identification means issued in another Member State shall be recognised in the first Member State for the purposes of cross-border authentication for that service online, provided that … (a) the electronic identification means is issued under an electronic identification scheme that is included in the list published by the Commission pursuant to Article 9; (b) the assurance level of the electronic identification means corresponds to an assurance level equal to or higher than the assurance level required by the relevant public sector body …

So the obligation bites only where a Member State has notified a scheme, only for cross-border recognition, and only for a public-sector online service where national law *already* requires electronic identification. Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/1502, `32015R1502`, `delegated`, `in-force`, supplies the technical criteria for identity proofing, credential management and authentication at each level — and exists solely to fill in the levels Art. 8 defines for notified schemes.

**The one hard floor is the wallet, and it is in the operative text rather than a recital.** Art. 5a(11) of the consolidated eIDAS:

> European Digital Identity Wallets shall be provided under an electronic identification scheme with assurance level high.

That is a real, mandatory, general-to-its-domain assurance requirement — and its domain is the wallet, not identification. Private-sector identification outside a notified scheme or a wallet, and domestic identification generally, are untouched by Arts. 6, 8 and 9.

**Two cautions.** The 2024 wallet implementing acts `32024R2977`, `32024R2979`, `32024R2980` and `32024R2982` are all `amended` by CIR (EU) 2026/1731 and no consolidated version is in the corpus, so the enrolment-at-level-high requirements they carry are **not quotable as current law from this corpus**; the quote above is taken from the consolidated eIDAS text instead, which is `in-force`. And `eidas-eudi`'s README Known Gap 2 records that the Art. 9 notification record — "where 'level of assurance high' acquires operational meaning" — is absent, so even the notified-scheme half cannot be checked against which schemes exist.

### 5.2 Thailand has taken the vocabulary into delegated legislation, and bound a licensing perimeter with it

Thailand is the only regime in the programme whose delegated legislation carries a NIST-lineage assurance framework: มธอ. ๑๑-๒๕๖๖, `mtho-11-2566-vol1-th`, `authority_tier: delegated`, `in-force`, defines ระดับความน่าเชื่อถือของการพิสูจน์ตัวตน (IAL) and ระดับความน่าเชื่อถือของการยืนยันตัวตน (AAL) and, as `thailand-id/findings/03` establishes over 38,439 characters with stated case folding, **names no wire format at all**.

**But the perimeter is a licence, not a population.** The Royal Decree's own title scopes it — "…that are Subject to Licensing" — and its s. 7, quoted from ETDA's official English `rd-did-2565-en` (`commentary`, `in-force`, `translation_status: official-non-authoritative`, `translation_of: rd-did-2565-th`; the Thai text is authentic and its *section numbers* are OCR-unreliable, which is why the English is used for the numbering):

> Section 7. The provision of a service relating to digital identification and authentication that possess any of the following characteristics shall be a service that is subject to licensing:
> (1) an identity proofing service;
> (2) an authenticator lifecycle management service
> (3) an authentication service;
> (4) a service for exchanging data for digital identification and authentication that is a network or system which connects and exchanges data relating to digital identification and authentication, excluding a service of an intermediary.

And s. 8 exempts, among others:

> (2) an identification and authentication service used by a person for his or her internal businesses without providing the service to external parties;

**An in-house identity check is expressly outside the regime.** That single exemption is dispositive for Q1: a rule that does not reach a party verifying identity for its own purposes is not a general assurance baseline, whatever vocabulary it uses.

s. 9 then restricts who may hold a licence to "a juristic person which is a limited company, a public limited company or other juristic person as specified by the Notification of the Commission".

**Known gap that bounds this answer:** only volume 1 of มธอ. ๑๑-๒๕๖๖ is held. Volumes 2 and 3 carry the actual IAL and AAL requirement texts and are absent in both languages, so *what a given level requires* is not supportable from this corpus — only that levels exist and whom they bind.

## 6. Utah, California, Singapore — and a qualification Utah's own findings owe

### 6.1 California: no baseline, and the absence is structural

Over `../ccpa/corpus` (46 items) and `../ccpa/corpus-regs` (91 items, `--in-force-only`): `documentary evidence` → 0/0, `proof of identity` → 0/0, `identity proofing` → 0/0, `government-issued` → 0 in the statute and 3 in the regulations. All three regulation hits are narrow: two are *redaction* rules forbidding a business to disclose an SSN or driver's-licence number in a response, and one is the parent/guardian check for a child's request, which itself requires the identification to be "deleted by the business from its records promptly after such verification is complete".

California's entire assurance apparatus is the graded requester-verification standard of Q4 — reasonable / reasonably high certainty, keyed to which right is exercised. **Layer not searched, and it is the one that matters:** California's identity-document law lives in the Vehicle Code and the Government Code, entirely outside CCPA/CPRA and outside this repo's declared scope. The CCPA corpus can say that *the CCPA* sets no baseline. It cannot say California does not.

### 6.2 Singapore: a general reliability standard with no level in it, and a national ID scheme that regulates issuance only

`assurance` returns 3 lines in 2 items across `../singapore-id/corpus`, all inside the ETA certification-authority regime — digital-signature reliability, not person-identity assurance. There is no IAL/AAL-shaped instrument anywhere in the corpus.

The nearest general provision is ETA 2010 s. 16O(1), `ETA2010`, `legislative`, `in-force`, which is open-textured by design:

> (a) as reliable as appropriate for the fulfilment of the function for which the method is being used, in the light of all the relevant circumstances, which may include — … (vi) the existence of a declaration by a supervisory body, an accreditation body or a voluntary scheme … or (vii) any applicable industry standard; or (b) proven in fact …

A reliability standard with no prescribed level, satisfiable by evidence alone. The National Registration Act 1965 compels *issuance* of an identity card; no provision requires any third party to proof anyone to any level.

**And the corpus states no legal basis for Singpass.** `Singpass` → **0**, `Myinfo` → **0**, `National Digital Identity` → **0** over the 20 items of `corpus/`; the same string returns 6 hits in `corpus-specs` (the live discovery document), which is the positive control proving the query works. The repo's README says why: the Singpass Services Agreement is behind a portal login and is "the document doing most of the regulating". **Stated plainly: this corpus cannot say what statute Singpass rests on, because none is in it.**

### 6.3 Utah — and the 2026 chapter its own findings do not cite

Utah's answer stands: no general baseline, assurance set interaction by interaction, and the verifier is usually a deputised private party — notary, retailer, licensee — rather than the state. Six findings in `../utah-id-law/findings/` converge on it.

**But the corpus now contains a statute the findings do not reach.** Title 63A Chapter 20, **State-Endorsed Digital Identity**, enacted by Chapter 436 of the 2026 General Session, is in `corpus/utah-code/C63A_2021050520210701.xml.gz`, and § 63A-20-303 is the most baseline-shaped text anywhere in the Utah corpus:

> The department shall establish and maintain identity proofing requirements for the issuance of a state-endorsed digital identity that: follow a generally accepted identity proofing standard; are commensurate with the risks of impersonation, fraud, and misuse associated with the credential; and are consistent with the privacy, civil liberties, and security requirements of this chapter. The identity proofing process shall be designed to establish, at a minimum, that: the applicant is a real individual; the applicant is the individual the applicant claims to be; the applicant's birth date is the date the applicant claims it to be; and the applicant meets the eligibility requirements of Section 63A-20-302.

That is IAL2-shaped language in a statute — and § 63A-20-302(7)(a) attaches a ceiling to it that no other proofing provision in the programme carries:

> The department may not require collection of information that is not necessary to verify identity or eligibility.

**The two searchable mentions of SEDI in `../utah-id-law/findings/` are not about this chapter.** `fishing-license-probe.md:290` uses "SEDI" to mean the Bakobo programme — "For SEDI purposes the practical upshot is that Utah has **no general identity-assurance baseline** to inherit" — and `interaction-survey.md` lists SEDI only as a topic in a per-title table. Neither cites § 63A-20-303 or § 63A-20-302.

**The conclusion survives, and the reasoning has to change.** Chapter 20 is not a general baseline: it governs the issuance of one opt-in credential, and the chapter expressly declines to compel its acceptance — a relying party "may accept a state-endorsed digital identity as proof of an individual's identity or identity attributes unless a different method of proof" is required. § 63A-20-601(3): "A relying party may accept a state-endorsed digital identity as proof of an individual's identity or identity attributes unless a different method of proof is required by law." So Utah still has no baseline for interactions. But "Utah has no general identity-assurance baseline to inherit" was written without the chapter that supplies precisely the proofing standard a SEDI programme would inherit, and the sentence should now read as being about *use*, not about *the standard*. Recorded as tick `~4hx6` in this repo's ledger, because the correction is owed by a repo this finding may not write to.

## 7. The comparison

| Regime | General provision? | Who is bound, to do what, when | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Utah** | **No.** § 63A-20-303 is proofing for one opt-in credential's issuance; R708-41 for driver-licence issuance | Nobody generally; enumerated actors per interaction, often deputised private parties | legislative + delegated |
| **EU (eIDAS)** | **No.** Art. 8(1) scopes the levels to notified schemes; Art. 6 to cross-border public-sector access | Member States that notify; wallet providers, at level high (Art. 5a(11)) | legislative + delegated |
| **CCPA** | **No.** Zero hits for the proofing phrase family | Businesses, verifying requesters only, graded by right | legislative + delegated |
| **Japan** | **No.** 番号法 §16 binds a defined class at a defined moment | 個人番号利用事務等実施者, verifying the person **supplying** a number, on collection | legislative + delegated + unpublished 主務省令 |
| **Singapore** | **No.** ETA s. 16O is reliability with no level; NRA 1965 compels issuance only | Nobody, to any level. Singpass has no statutory basis in this corpus | legislative |
| **Thailand** | **No.** IAL/AAL exist in delegated legislation; RD s. 8(2) exempts in-house verification | Four enumerated licensable service types, Thai juristic persons only | delegated |
| **Indonesia** | **Closest to yes.** Compulsory registration + a registrar's duty to verify + NIK as the universal key | Instansi Pelaksana, proofing at registration, once. Downstream: assertion | legislative (all `amended` in corpus) |
| **India** | **No.** Strong enrolment proofing; use voluntary by s. 4(3); compulsion only via another Act, s. 4(7) | UIDAI/registrars at enrolment; requesting entities may authenticate, nobody must | legislative + delegated + judicial |

**The taxonomy earns its keep here.** Three of the nine — Indonesia, India, and Utah's new Chapter 20 — have genuine IAL2-shaped **proofing**, and none of them has a general **authentication** requirement to go with it. Japan has neither at general scope. The regimes that look most impressive on Q1 from a distance are impressive at the front door and silent thereafter, and a comparison that did not separate proofing from authentication would have reported three yeses.

## 8. What this finding does not establish

- **Nothing about any regime's sectoral rules.** Banking KYC, telecoms SIM registration and equivalents are outside every one of the nine corpora, and they are where a de facto baseline most plausibly lives.
- **Nothing about Indonesia's ministerial layer**, which carries the KTP-el and IKD specifications and is unreachable from this box.
- **Nothing about what a Thai IAL or AAL level requires**, because volumes 2 and 3 of มธอ. ๑๑-๒๕๖๖ are not held.
- **Nothing about Japan's APPI**, which is out of scope by decision.
- **Nothing about which EU schemes are notified**, which is `eidas-eudi`'s Known Gap 2 and is where level high acquires operational meaning.
- **Nothing current about Indonesia's Adminduk family**, every item of which the corpus marks `amended`.
